### What OpenClaw and Hermes actually are

- **OpenClaw** is a free, MIT-licensed, self-hosted autonomous agent (TypeScript) by Peter Steinberger, first released 24 Nov 2025, now under the OpenClaw Foundation. It connects LLMs to WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack and Discord and can run shell commands, browse and manage files. Site: openclaw.ai, repo github.com/openclaw/openclaw, skill marketplace ClawHub.
- **openclaw.com is not the project.** It was registered in 2014 and currently serves a bio page for a Cairo law-firm partner. The portfolio's OpenClaw case study was linking to it; that link has been removed and the card reframed as work built on the open-source OpenClaw.
- **Hermes Agent** is Nous Research's open-source (MIT, Python) agent runtime, created Jul 2025, ~252K stars: github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent.

## Overall verdict

**35 claims checked: 20 confirmed, 13 partly right, 2 contradicted.** Nothing central is invented; the thesis (services money is moving to AI deployment, security and operation; freelance platforms are consolidating toward bigger, badged buyers; outcome pricing beats hourly) survives verification. The brief's weaknesses are attribution drift, stale figures and secondary sources inflating headlines.

**Remove before using the brief with anyone else**

1. The Anthropic "CFO" quote about demand "outpacing any single delivery model" — it does not exist.
2. "56% of freelancers get work through networks, up from 30% in 2024" — a German IT-freelancer survey with no such baseline.
3. "AI cut freelance rates ~30%" — the source measured job-posting volume, not rates.
4. Mercor as bridge income — Mercor explicitly does not onboard Pakistan-based experts.

**Replace**

5. "220,000+ exposed OpenClaw instances" → "40,000+ in the first 24 hours of scanning" (SecurityScorecard, Feb 2026).
6. "$364M → $59B AI security services by 2030" → "securing-AI market ~$4.8B by 2027" (Gartner, Aug 2026).
7. Gartner $6.15T / +10.8% → $6.31T / +13.5% (April 2026 revision).
8. Endava guidance −3.5% to −1.5% → −6% to −5% (May 2026), with results delayed after an accounting review.
9. "Partner-led deployments succeed about twice as often" → "about three times as often" (Fortune/MIT).
10. "97% of firms lack AI controls" → "97% of organisations that suffered an AI breach lacked access controls" (IBM 2025), or use IBM 2026: 92% of breached, 40% overall.

**Treat as indicative only**

- All pricing: the Space-O OpenClaw pricing page is dead (404); Devox's rescue tiers are unattributed "published ranges"; agency setup quotes found elsewhere run $1,200–2,600 one-off (Monocubed) plus $20–60/month hosting. Reply-rate and white-label margin numbers are vendor estimates, as the brief itself says.
- Vendor surveys: Augmentt (n=193), GoodFirms (100+ companies), Kaseya (vendor-run).
- P@SHA figures: consistently reported by three outlets but the underlying report is not published.

**Implications for the three bets**

- Bet 1 (agent and MCP security) is supported by real, primary evidence of exposure (40K+ instances, 341+ malicious skills, 53% static credentials in open-source MCP code) and by OWASP's new Agentic Top 10. The competitive field is moving fast (SecurityScorecard, Koi now inside Palo Alto Networks), so differentiation has to come from published proof: hardening guides, a config scanner, before/after reports.
- Bet 2 (white-label Copilot/Azure bench) is supported by the strongest data in the brief: 30M+ paid Copilot seats, 3% of MSPs fully Copilot-ready, 48% of MSPs naming AI the top client need with 13% monetising it. It requires demonstrable Microsoft 365 security skills; the AB-900 and SC-401 exams exist (AB-650 is still beta).
- Bet 3 (rescue and run) rests on the weakest numbers (Devox tiers, HN anecdotes) but on a sound mechanism: Goldman's 76% adoption vs 14% integration gap and MIT's 95% pilot failure rate are confirmed.
